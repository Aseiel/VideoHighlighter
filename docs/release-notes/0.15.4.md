# VideoHighlighter 0.15.4

<!-- Draft, kept up to date as fixes land; the version number is provisional. Check each line against the app before publishing. -->

## Fixed

- **Subtitles no longer crash the app on an AMD graphics card.** On a machine with a Radeon, 0.15.3 closed without a message as soon as transcription started: OpenVINO listed the AMD card, and Whisper was compiled on it as if it were an Intel one, which crashed below anything the app could catch. OpenVINO is now used only on a card that reports itself as Intel. Elsewhere Whisper runs on the processor, and the frame encoder on DirectML or the processor.

## Timeline

Clips that were in the timeline but could not be seen, reported after resizing panels. Four separate causes, each fixed:

- **A rebuild kept squaring the zoom.** Toggling a filter, a layer or a search result rebuilt the timeline and multiplied its zoom by itself. Over three rebuilds a film shrank from 0.66 to 0.03 of its width, a few pixels with nothing visible on it. A rebuild now keeps the zoom it had.
- **The timeline opened on the middle of the film** with the playhead at 0, so the clips at the start were out of sight. It now opens where the playhead is.
- **The edit timeline opened centred too.** With one or two short clips none of them showed. It now opens on its first clip.
- **The panel between the two timelines could be dragged shut**, leaving the edit timeline 0 pixels tall. Neither can be closed that way now.
- **The wheel zooms between the whole video and a frame's width**, and no further either way.
- **The viewer fits a laptop screen.** At 1366×728 the timeline was 98 pixels wide and the window was taller than the screen. The timeline now gets 360 pixels and the window fits.

## Shots

- **Black bars no longer hide cuts.** Letterbox and pillarbox bars never change, so they watered down every frame-to-frame difference. On a film given bars, shot detection lost 45 to 70 of about 300–435 real cuts, and the chapter pass found 150 shots instead of 333. The picture inside the bars is now found from bright frames in the middle of the video, and only the picture is read. On the same film, the cuts lost to the bars went to none.

## Thanks

- **Scott Napier**, whose requests made the frame-accurate shot list and subtitles timed to shot changes. On a 20-minute episode the shot list finds 436 shots in 46 seconds. Subtitle lines starting on a cut went from 17 to 115, and lines running into the next shot went from 55 to 12.
