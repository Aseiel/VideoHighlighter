# VideoHighlighter 0.13.2

Making the dataset becomes part of the app. 0.13 could train a model once you had example clips; 0.13.2 starts a step earlier, from a single video and nothing else: it cuts the footage, crops it to the people in it, puts look-alike samples together, and you name them group by group, move what landed in the wrong place, and check the rest. A Health page then says whether the dataset is ready to train and how often the automatic sorting was right. Around that, the action trainer can fine-tune SigLIP2 itself, the preview plays high-resolution video at full speed with live face boxes that keep up, and on-demand analysis and composition rules work again with a custom detector.

14 commits on main since 0.13.1.

## Known in this release

- **"Download and install" is not offered yet.** Only a build that carries the release signing key offers it, and this one does not. The update banner still tells you about new versions and opens the download page.
- **Datasets is new and still experimental.** The steps and the files they write may change in the next release.
- **Rules from objects found need a detector you supply,** and they are only as good as it is. With a detector that finds its objects in about a quarter of the frames where they are, two rules measured on 1,890 hand-sorted clips were right 63% and 22% of the time, so they stay suggestions. Nothing is decided by a rule until people have checked it and it has proven right.

## Datasets

The new first tab under Train: the datasets you have, **New dataset…**, and five steps for the one you open. Every button runs one `python -m modules.teach` command, so each step can also be scripted.

- **Videos.** Add files, a folder or a link, then **Cut into samples and group them**. The footage is cut into 5-second samples and, for actions, cropped to the people in it (on by default for new datasets).
- **A crop is a sample.** A shot often holds two people doing two different things, and a sample has one label. With cropping on, each person's crop becomes a sample of its own, grouped, checked and trained on by itself. The whole shots are kept, never trained on.
- **Groups.** Samples that look alike are shown together, the tightest groups first. Click the tiles that do not belong and name the rest, or mark a group "nothing interesting" or "mixed", and split a mixed group into smaller ones. Only the tiles you were shown are labelled; the rest of each group is checked later. Look-alike samples are often one scene rather than one action, so a group never labels what nobody looked at.
- **Browse.** Every group, class, "nothing", "unusable" and the samples not decided yet, as folders of tiles. Select (Shift-click for a range) and move them to a class, a new class, nothing, unusable, back to not decided, or another group. The dataset's folder is in the header, each sample's file is in its tooltip, and Copy paths copies the selected ones.
- **Check guesses.** The review window from 0.13, now fed by what you named: each tile shows its guess, you click the wrong ones.
- **Health.** A line per check, each with what to do: every group named, auto-accepted samples spot-checked, enough samples per class (5, or training leaves the class out; 20 to be solid), each class from 3 or more videos, no one video most of a class, a "none of these" class. One video gives at best "needs more videos", and says how many more each class needs.
- **How good was the sorting.** Of the samples you decided, how often the guess you were shown was right, by where it came from (a group, the sort, auto-accept, a rule) and by class, with what the wrong ones really were.
- **Rules from objects found.** For classes that look alike but differ in how many of something there are, or what touches what: point the dataset at your own detector, let it find objects in every sample, and write rules in the composition engine's format, so a rules file made for the timeline can be reused. A rule's guess goes to review like any other, and a rule decides alone only after 10 checks at 95% right, under the same spot checks as auto-accept. The detector is used where it is, never copied into a build, and nothing is trained on it.
- `docs/TEACH-A-MODEL.md` covers the commands: `group`, `name-group`, `move`, `health`, `projects`, `detect` and `rules`.

## Action models

- **Fine-tune SigLIP2 in the trainer.** Train → Actions has a checkbox to train SigLIP2's top layers together with the action head, on an Intel Arc or NVIDIA card. Clips are decoded once into a frame cache kept for the next run, the epoch is chosen on videos the model never saw, and the fine-tuned model is kept only if it beats the plain one on them. On a 2,695-clip dataset (Arc A750, 71 minutes) it was right first time on 59% of held-out clips against 52%, and sorted 59% with confidence against 44%.
- **Action points count with no actions typed.** A blank action list now runs the action pass over every category the installed model knows, instead of skipping it when the points are set.
- **A cached run with no actions is filled in.** A video whose cached action pass came back empty is run again and the actions saved, instead of scoring zero for actions until run again on demand.

## Timeline and preview

- **A checkbox per action category** on the ACTIONS layer, with its bar count at the current merge gap, and show all / none.
- **◀ steps back.** One click returns to the start of the current group; a double-click goes to the group before, also during playback.
- **High-resolution video plays at full speed.** The preview converted every frame at the source's full resolution on the interface thread, about 85 ms a frame on a 7K source, so playback painted 10 frames a second. Frames are now shrunk to the size shown on two background threads first: on a 7K side-by-side clip, 10 → 30 fps.
- **Live face boxes keep up with the picture.** Faces are still detected about twice a second, but a tracker now follows each one on every displayed frame. On the same clip the boxes update 28 times a second instead of 2, and trail the picture by about 0 ms instead of 230 ms.

## Chat

- **Use Ollama on another PC.** "Other PC setup…" shows the commands to run on the machine with Ollama (Windows, Linux or macOS) so this one can reach it, with a Copy button and a connection test.
- **Long requests no longer fail.** A request bigger than the 2,048-token context grows the context to fit, up to the model's own limit, and a refusal gives Ollama's reason rather than "400 Bad Request". With no server answering, the model list stays empty instead of showing names nothing can run.

## Fixes

- **Objects on demand uses the detector you picked.** The main window saved its settings only when it closed, and on-demand analysis reads them from `config.yaml`, so a custom object model chosen in Advanced was used by a full run but not on demand, which ran the standard model and found none of the custom classes. The settings are now saved when you pick a model, when the timeline opens and when a run starts.
- **Run Rules runs.** It stopped at once with "unknown signal kind: composition". It now applies the saved rules (saving an edited table first) and fetches what they read: a detection pass for the classes the cache lacks, and the measured signals. Objects on demand now keeps the boxes it found, which is what the rules and the overlay read.
- **Auto-crop splits two people doing two things.** 0.13.1 merged two crops that shared people into one and then kept the clip whole, so in close footage two actions side by side stayed together (split in 1 of 18 reviewed windows). Two planned crops are no longer merged: on one 52-minute video, 220 of 623 shots are split into a crop per person, against 37. `SAME_ACTION_OVERLAP` in `modules/crop/config.py` brings the merge back.
